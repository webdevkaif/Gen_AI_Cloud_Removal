The Challenge of Cloud Cover in Optical Remote Sensing

The North Eastern Region (NER) of India is characterized by lush tropical rain forests, mountainous topography, and an extended monsoon season. This results in persistent, near-permanent cloud cover for up to 80% of the year, rendering conventional optical satellite imagery (like LISS-IV) unusable for critical applications.

CLEAR_SEE presents a multi-modal Generative AI framework that reconstructs optical details underneath heavy cloud contamination by fusing LISS-IV imagery with Sentinel-1 SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) data (which penetrates clouds) and temporal references, producing cloud-free, spectrally consistent reconstructions.

Framework Architecture at a Glance

Multi-Modal Fusion

Combines optical LISS-IV visual bands with cloud-penetrating Sentinel-1 SAR C-band microwave radar data to reconstruct physical ground geometry.

Generative AI Core

Leverages Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) and multi-modal transformers with cross-attention to synthesize fine textures and preserve spatial resolution.

Downstream LULC Enhancement

Restores the semantic consistency of land cover. Cloud shadows no longer misclassify as water bodies; cloud peaks no longer hide infrastructure.

The Four Fundamental Pillars of the Cloud Problem

Comprehensive scientific breakdown curated by AeroSense-10 on why cloud contamination remains the single hardest bottleneck in optical earth observation.

01

Atmospheric Mie Scattering

Cloud droplets (diameter 10–30 µm) are significantly larger than visible light wavelengths (0.4–0.9 µm). Non-selective Mie scattering scatters 99.8% of photons in all directions, turning the atmosphere into a solid white diffuse reflector and suffocating surface radiance.

Physical Extinction: T(x) ≈ 0
02

The Temporal Invalidation Trap

Traditional compositing replaces clouds with clear images from prior months. But in monsoonal NER, clear passes don't happen for 4–5 months. By then, crops have shifted, floodwaters have moved, and infrastructure has changed—making past images useless for real-time monitoring.

Temporal Lag: 90–150 Days
03

Cloud Shadow Spectral Mimicry

Thick cloud shadows plunge surface irradiance to near zero. In VNIR bands, cloud shadows mimic open water and flooded fields with identical low reflectance. Automated classification models report massive false flood disasters, misguiding emergency response teams.

False Water Rate: Up to 74%
04

AI Hallucination vs Physical Grounding

When pure generative models (like unconditional GANs) are tasked to inpaint huge cloud occlusions, they invent fictitious villages, fake roads, and nonexistent rivers. The challenge is strictly conditioning generative diffusion on cloud-penetrating Sentinel-1 SAR microwave radar cues.

LISS-IV Sensor Specifications

The LISS-IV (Linear Imaging Self Scanning Sensor-IV) is a high-resolution multispectral camera onboard ISRO's Resourcesat-2/2A satellites. It operates in three spectral bands:

Spectral Band Spectral Range Spatial Resolution
Band 2 (Green) 0.52 - 0.59 µm 5.8 meters (Nadir)
Band 3 (Red) 0.62 - 0.68 µm 5.8 meters (Nadir)
Band 4 (NIR) 0.77 - 0.86 µm 5.8 meters (Nadir)

While 5.8m resolution provides exceptional clarity for agricultural parcels and urban grids, LISS-IV lacks a Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR) or thermal band, which are traditionally used for thin cloud penetration and cloud-shadow mask extraction. Hence, Generative AI models are crucial to infer surface features.

Auxiliary Sensor Guidance (Sentinel-1 SAR)

Sentinel-1 is a C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) satellite mission. Unlike optical sensors, C-band microwaves (wavelength ~5.6 cm) penetrate cloud layers, dust, and rain without scattering.

Polarimetric SAR Features

Our model utilizes dual polarization: VV (Vertical-transmit, Vertical-receive) which is highly sensitive to surface roughness, water body shorelines, and soil moisture; and VH (Vertical-transmit, Horizontal-receive) which is sensitive to volume scattering from vegetation canopies.

By inputting the SAR co-polarized and cross-polarized bands into the cross-attention blocks of the generator, the model establishes solid boundary conditions for roads, forests, and rivers, avoiding hallucination and stabilizing generative textures.

  • Midground (Bushes & Fence): Moderate displacement.
